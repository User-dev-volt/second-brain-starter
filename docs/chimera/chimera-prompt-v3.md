# Project Chimera — World & Systems Prompt (v3)

Build the world, the magic system and the narrative spine for a **dark action-fantasy game**.

This document supersedes v2. v2 is archived at `_archive/chimera-prompt-v2.md` and the bible it
produced at `_archive/v2-narrative-bible.md`. Neither is canon. Do not mine them for content, tone,
vocabulary or characters. They exist so nobody repeats the mistake described below.

---

## 0. HOW TO RUN THIS PROMPT — READ BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

**This is a gated build, not a one-shot generation.**

v2 was run as one shot. It produced 29,000 finished words in a register that made the magic
unplayable, and the problem was only visible after everything had been built on top of it. The
cost of a wrong bedrock decision is every word written after it.

**There are six gates. At each one you stop and a human decides.**

At every gate:

- Write **no more than ~1,200 words** before stopping.
- Present **2–4 options that differ in kind**, not three flavours of one idea. If your options
  could all be true at once, they are not options.
- One short paragraph each: what it is, what it makes possible, what it costs. Name the cost
  honestly — an option with no downside is one you have not understood.
- State **your recommendation** and why, in one sentence.
- Then **stop.** Do not write the next section. Do not "assume and continue." Do not deliver the
  next three gates "for context."
- If you hit a decision that is not on the gate list but would be expensive to reverse later,
  **raise it at the nearest gate** instead of deciding it quietly.

**The diagnostic rule:** any choice that changes how magic *feels in the player's hands* is a gate
item, never an authorial decision. v2 failed because it optimised for prose quality against a brief
that never once said the words *game*, *player*, *combat*, or *fast*.

---

## 1. WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

A dark action-fantasy world for a game with **combat as a primary verb**.

Magic is a **combat verb first and a story engine second**. Both matter, in that order. A rule that
reads beautifully on the page and cannot be executed by a player under pressure is a broken rule.

**The pitch of the system, one line:** matter is not permanent, and a trained person can loosen it
and re-fix it — in the open, in a fight, in about a second. The reason the world is not run by gods
is that both halves of that sentence have a price.

**Tone target:** real stakes, real cost, real horror — but the protagonists are *capable*, fights
are *exciting*, and power is *fun to use* before it turns out to be terrible. Cost lands in
consequences and character, not in making magic tedious to perform. Nobody kneels on a floor for
four hours. Reference weight class: *Arcane*, *Fullmetal Alchemist*, *Berserk*, *Jujutsu Kaisen* —
not literary tragedy, not cosy fantasy.

---

## 2. LOCKED BEDROCK — decided by the human, do not renegotiate

These were chosen deliberately. Build on them. Do not reopen them, improve them, or quietly soften
them. If you believe one is wrong, say so in one sentence at the next gate and then build it anyway.

**2.1 — The core mechanic is SOLVE & SET, a two-stroke.**
Every working is two motions. **SOLVE**: loosen matter out of its fixed state into a workable one.
**SET**: fix it into the new form. Charge pays for the loosening. Substance bounds what the result
can be. Speed comes from chaining both strokes into a single gesture. **Half-set matter — solved but
never set — is a real, persistent, dangerous state in the world**, not a metaphor. Everything else
in the system descends from this.

**2.2 — Substance comes from four legitimate sources, and all four are in play.**
Carried reagents (vials, powders, filings, salt, wire, glass). Ripped from the surroundings (stone,
rust, rain, mud, the iron in a railing). The caster's own body. Pre-loaded charges bound into
objects during downtime. These are not tiers of power and none is "the real one" — they are
different answers with different risks, and they are how casters differ from each other.

**2.3 — Charge exists in three topologies at once.**
It is **in the land**, unevenly, with rich zones that raise a caster's reserve, regeneration and
throughput — map control is power. It is **carriable**, tapped in advance and held in charged stone
or fluid — flasks, cored stones, supply lines, theft. And it can be **built into a person**, through
implants and augmentation, so that some casters carry their reserve inside their body. All three
coexist and they interact.

**2.4 — There is no single casting method. There are schools.**
Handling and breaking material, spoken forms, contact casting, projection, inscribed and drawn
figures — all valid. Every caster is *skilled in their own way*, and that phrase must be given
structure: it means **distinct schools with distinct silhouettes, strengths and counters**, not a
world where everyone can do anything however they like. Mush is the failure mode here. See Gate 2.

**2.5 — Register is apothecary and chemical, kept down to earth.**
Reagents, reactions, purity, dosage, volatility, contamination, residue, solvent, precipitate.
**"Transmutation" is our word and is used freely.** An astral or celestial layer may be blended in
if it earns its place and stays grounded — but it must never become the explanation for how things
work. The physical layer is the real one.

**2.6 — The FMA ban from v2 is lifted.**
Circles, arrays, drawn figures, ranks, implants, the word transmutation — all available if they
serve the game. **In exchange, the system's originality must be carried by SOLVE & SET and by the
three-way charge topology.** Those are the things that make this not a re-skin. Lean on them hard,
and make sure that anything a player finds familiar behaves differently here than they expect.

**2.7 — Clean slate on story.** No character, place, term, event or phrase from v2 survives.

## 3. THE SYSTEM YOU MUST BUILD

The two-axis structure is the spine. **Do not collapse it into one.** A caster can fail either axis
independently, and the two failures feel completely different.

### 3.1 The two strokes
Define SOLVE and SET precisely enough to teach an apprentice in an afternoon. For each: what it
costs, how long it takes, what it looks like, what happens if it is interrupted mid-motion, and
what a skilled practitioner does differently from a desperate one. Say clearly which stroke is the
hard one and why — they should not be symmetrical.

### 3.2 Axis one — SUBSTANCE. You cannot get out what was never in.
A working rearranges what is physically present. To make steel you need what steel is made of.
Skill does not substitute for absent material and neither does raw power. Define how a caster
*knows* what is in reach — the read, the sense, the tell — because in a fight that read has to
happen in under a second and it has to be possible to be wrong about it.

### 3.3 Axis two — CHARGE. The loosening has to be paid for.
Charge is what SOLVE spends. Per **2.3** it lives in the land, in carriers, and in bodies. Define:
how a caster draws from each, how fast, how they know they are running low, what running out feels
like mid-stroke, how charge is moved and stored, and who controls the supply. Make the three
topologies genuinely interact — a caster standing in a rich zone should play differently from one
running on a flask, and both differently from one running on implants.

### 3.4 The four failure modes
Rules, never randomness, and teachable in one breath. Define all four precisely and consistently:

1. **It fails to solve.** Nothing loosens. Charge is spent anyway. The lucky outcome.
2. **It solves but never sets.** The dangerous new one. Matter comes loose and stays loose. Say
   exactly what half-set matter *is*, how it behaves, how long it lasts, whether it spreads, what
   it does to anything that touches it, and how it is cleaned up — and make it a hazard that
   persists in the world and on the battlefield after the caster has moved on.
3. **It sets wrong.** Enough charge, not enough substance, nothing else in reach. The working
   completes out of what is actually present: technically correct and wrong. Give warping
   consistent rules so a player can predict it and dread it.
4. **It reaches.** The working makes up the shortfall from the nearest source that contains what it
   needs, and does not care what that source is. This is where the horror lives and where the
   antagonist's whole edifice comes from.

### 3.5 Overdraw
Say what pulling too hard does to the caster and to the land. Both permanent, both observable in
stages, both readable by another character across a room. Implants change this — say how.

### 3.6 The dead
Bringing back the dead must be a specific, terrible, consistent consequence of the above, not a
special rule. A body contains almost everything a person is made of and is missing exactly the
thing that matters. Nothing ever comes back. Say precisely what happens instead, and make it a
fixed documented phenomenon rather than a monster.

## 4. PLAY REQUIREMENTS — THESE OUTRANK EVERY LITERARY CONSIDERATION

If a section of your output cannot satisfy these, the section is wrong, however well it reads.

**4.1 Three speeds, and every school has all three.**
A **fast** working — roughly one second, the bread-and-butter verb, usable while moving. A
**heavy** working — several seconds of commitment, needs cover or an ally, and the enemy can see it
coming. A **prepared** working — minutes to hours, done outside combat, and it is how downtime pays
off inside combat.

**4.2 Counterplay is a first-class requirement.**
How do you interrupt a caster mid-stroke? How do you deny them substance? How do you drain, block
or poison their charge? What does a non-caster do about a caster, and why are non-casters still
dangerous? A magic system with no answers here produces boring fights.

**4.3 Legibility.**
An observer must be able to tell what just happened and roughly what it cost. Define what is
*visible* from outside: what the strokes look like, what half-set matter looks like, what a
draining caster looks like, what a rich zone looks like. If two schools look the same on screen,
one of them is redundant.

**4.4 Improvisation.**
A player must be able to look at an unfamiliar room and see three options. The system should reward
noticing what a space is made of. Give worked examples of the same caster solving the same problem
differently in a forge, a flooded cellar, and a bare stone cell.

**4.5 Progression.**
How does a caster get stronger over a campaign, given that raw power is not the axis? Reagent
quality, school technique, implants, charge infrastructure, reading speed, stroke-chaining — say
which of these are the real progression axes and which are traps.

**4.6 Utility outside combat.**
The world must run on this. Industry, medicine, construction, agriculture, war. If transmutation
only ever appears in fights, the setting is a fighting game with scenery.

## 5. THE GATES

Stop at every one. Present options. Wait.

### GATE 1 — The strokes, the Law, and the words for it
Deliver: SOLVE and SET fully specified; the two axes; the four failure modes; overdraw; what
happens to the dead. Plus **the Law in one sentence a working caster could say out loud** — short,
rhythmic, memorable, the kind of thing said under the breath before a hard working. Plus the naming
and vocabulary key: **at most ten invented terms in the entire world**, each earning its place.
**Decide with the human:** the exact wording of the Law; the name of the charge; whether an astral
layer is in and how far; the top-level vocabulary.

### GATE 2 — The schools
Deliver: **four or five schools**, each defined by its source (per 2.2), its casting method (per
2.4), which stroke it is better at, its silhouette in a fight, its three speeds, what it is bad at,
and which school beats it and why. This is the class system; it is where "everyone has their own
way" becomes structure. Include at least one school built on implants and at least one that is
outright disreputable.
**Decide with the human:** which schools are playable, which are enemy-only, and which one the
protagonist starts in.

### GATE 3 — The loophole and the small atrocity
Deliver: the single exploit that breaks the Law, following from its exact wording with the
inevitability of a real loophole — and **it must be impossible to perform alone**, so that it
structurally requires other people to pay. Since bodies are one of the four sources, "use people"
is the obvious idea; the lazy version is worthless. What makes it work is a **specific, limited,
defensible-sounding form of it that a decent person could sign off on**, and that does not read as
murder to the people doing it. Explaining it must take **five sentences or fewer**.
Then: **one intimate, non-spectacular horror**, early, involving someone of no strategic importance
— not a mentor, not a relative, not a plot key, never avenged. A *correct* application of the
system, not a villain's cruelty. Nobody in the scene is sadistic. The reader follows the reasoning
and finds no error in it. Quiet and procedural — no gore, no torture, no child harmed for shock.
**Decide with the human:** the form of the loophole before anything is built on it.

### GATE 4 — The antagonist
Deliver: **three candidate antagonists.** Each achieved his current state by cutting something out
of himself — a real, operable component the system recognises — and each procedure must be
rule-legal: anyone with his knowledge and nerve could do it, and *the reason almost nobody does is
the interesting part*. Say what he removed, what he became, and what he still wants that proves the
removal failed. His goal must be sympathetic if you squint; many players should privately agree
with him for a chapter. He is not sealed and not prophesied. **He does not get stronger across the
story** — if anything he is becoming less able. The fights get harder because of what he has
*built*, not because of what he *is*.
**Decide with the human:** which candidate, and what gets grafted from the other two.

### GATE 5 — The world and the apparatus
Deliver: the nation, the geography, the culture, the names — and **the apparatus**: how the country
itself is the machine. Infrastructure or institution as working hardware, running for generations
in plain sight, doing genuinely useful ordinary work. The people maintaining it are mostly not
conspirators; **the horror is competence, not conspiracy.** Include **two historical events** the
player will first accept as ordinary history and later recognise as construction steps. For each:
the schoolbook version, the detail that never fit, what it actually accomplished, and the moment it
flips.
Plus **the righteous enemy**: a survivor of one of those events who hunts the protagonists using
the same forbidden art, with a grievance the story never fully refutes. Not the antagonist's
servant, not secretly good, not converted. They use the loophole knowingly and are honest about who
pays, by a rule they follow rather than an appetite. At the end the player should still think they
were substantially right and be unable to say cleanly what they should have done instead.
**Decide with the human:** the two historical events, and the righteous enemy's rule.

### GATE 6 — The lieutenants and the endgame
Deliver: **seven lieutenants**, each an externalised piece of the antagonist. Use whatever taxonomy
the world already implies — trades, schools, stages of a working, seasons, ranks. Each is a
*fightable boss with a distinct mechanical identity* tied to its school. Give each a death that
**argues against the thing it embodies**: the manner of dying refutes its own premise. **Nobody
dies to a bigger spell.** Several should die without the protagonists killing them; at least one
dies by being given exactly what it wanted; at least one dies because a piece cannot do a thing
only a whole person can do. Order them so the sequence is a **rising argument, not a rising power
curve**.
Then the endgame: the protagonist wins by **surrendering the power**. The shortcut must be real and
genuinely work — not a temptation that curdles on contact. The refusal must defeat the antagonist
thematically: the argument he built his life on is demonstrated false in front of him **by an
action, not a speech**, and there must be a mechanism by which a demonstrated argument actually
costs him. This is an action game, so the climax must also be a *fight worth playing* — the
thematic turn and the mechanical climax have to be the same event.
Show what the surrender costs: **at least four things, permanent**, including one the player was
rooting for and one that hurts someone who did not get a vote. And what it does not fix: **at least
five**, including the dead and the political reality. Some things get worse.
**Decide with the human:** the shortcut, and the shape of the final encounter.

## 6. REGISTER

**Use the language of substances and reactions**, kept concrete and physical: purity, dosage,
residue, solvent, precipitate, volatility, contamination, saturation, decay. Also the language of
trades that handle dangerous material for a living — foundry, refinery, dispensary, mine, field
hospital. This is a world where people who do this professionally talk about it the way chemists
and armourers talk about their work: precisely, unsentimentally, with dark humour.

**Banned entirely: the language of accounting, law and bureaucracy.** No ledgers, debts, arrears,
billing, credit, collateral, probate, filings, clerks, audits, dockets, statutes, quotas. Nobody's
magic is tracked in a book. If a sentence would fit in a solicitor's letter, cut it.

**Also banned: the domestic-kitchen register.** No pots, no simmering, no brewing, no bakery, no
"the shape without the substance" cookery metaphors. This is what killed v2. Transmutation is
industrial, medical and martial — not culinary.

**Keep it simple.** One central metaphor, held consistently. **At most ten invented terms in the
entire world.** I should be able to hold the whole vocabulary in my head after one read. If
explaining the loophole takes more than five sentences it is too complicated. Every faction-facing
name must be sayable out loud in two seconds. Concrete nouns, plain prose, no preciousness.

---

## 7. HARD CONSTRAINTS

- **No chosen-one prophecy. No sealed dark lord. No power-scaling escalation** — the endgame is not
  a bigger fight, it is a harder one for reasons that are thematic.
- The antagonist is defeated by a **thematic argument the story has been building since the first
  hour of play.**
- **Conservation is the law and I want it** — but the phrase *equivalent exchange* is banned, and so
  is stating conservation as a bare slogan about gain and price. Originality is carried by SOLVE &
  SET and the three-way charge topology.
- Do not reskin FFVII's Lifestream, Mistborn, Avatar's bending, Nasuverse magecraft, or Warhammer's
  Warp. Do not name the charge *mana*, *aether*, *chi*, *essence*, or *mako*.
- **Every rule must have teeth.** It must be able to hurt a sympathetic person, and it should
  generate grief on its own without anyone choosing to be cruel.
- **Every rule must also be fun.** If a rule only ever punishes, it is a tax, not a mechanic. The
  good ones should make a clever player feel clever.

---

## 8. CRAFT

Professional story-bible prose for adults. Concrete and unsentimental. Names must sound like one
coherent culture — state the naming key and hold to it. Where a rule is stated, state it once,
precisely, in a form that can be quoted back. Where a scene is given, make it playable: say what the
player is doing with their hands.

**And keep the gates.** Six stops. The human decides at each one. Nothing gets built on an
unapproved foundation ever again.
