# Project Chimera — Narrative Spine Prompt (v2)

Build an original good-vs-evil narrative spine for a world with magic.

## THE SYSTEM (two axes — this is the part that must be original)

Magic here is **transformation from a material source**. A practitioner takes hold of
existing matter and makes it into something else. Two separate things are required, and
they fail in two separate ways. This two-axis structure is the spine of the whole world —
do not collapse it into one.

**Axis 1 — SUBSTANCE. You cannot get out what was never in.**
A working rearranges what is physically present. To make wine you need what wine is made
of; water alone will not do it, and no amount of skill or power substitutes for absent
material. The practitioner must supply, or stand near, a source that genuinely contains
what the result requires. This is the law, and it is the world's moral spine.

**Axis 2 — ENERGY. The rearranging is powered by a life energy that flows through the
earth and pools underground.**
It behaves like groundwater: it has a table, it seeps, it can be tapped, drained, dammed
and fouled, and it recharges slowly. It looks bottomless because you can never see the
floor of a well — but it is local, finite and slow. Having enough material does not mean
you can afford the working; having enough energy does not mean the material is there.
The two constraints are independent and a practitioner can fail either one.

**THE THREE FAILURE MODES.** When a working demands more than the source can supply, it
does not politely stop. Define all three precisely and consistently — they are rules, never
randomness, and they must be teachable to an apprentice in a shed:

1. **It doesn't work.** The working fails cleanly. Nothing happens, the energy is spent,
   and this is the *lucky* outcome.
2. **It warps.** The working completes using what is actually present, producing something
   that is technically correct and wrong — the shape without the substance, food with no
   nourishment in it, a mended bone of the wrong material. Give the warping consistent
   rules so a reader can predict it and dread it.
3. **It reaches.** The dangerous one. The working makes up the shortfall from the nearest
   available source that contains what it needs — and does not care what that source is.
   This is where the horror lives and where the antagonist's whole edifice comes from.

**RESURRECTION.** Bringing back the dead must be a specific, terrible, consistent case of
the above. A body is a source that contains almost everything a person is made of and is
missing exactly the thing that matters, so the working always reaches, and always for the
wrong thing. Nothing ever comes back. Say precisely what happens instead and make it a
fixed, documented phenomenon rather than a monster.

**OVERDRAW.** Say what pulling too hard does to the practitioner and to the land, and make
both permanent and observable in stages.

## WHAT I WANT, IN THIS ORDER

**1. The Law and its loophole.**
State the cost rule in one sentence a peasant could recite — short, rhythmic, memorable,
the kind of thing said while working. Then state the single exploit that breaks it, and why
that exploit structurally requires other people to pay. The exploit must follow from the
Law's exact wording with the inevitability of a real loophole, and it must be impossible to
perform alone.

*A warning about the obvious solution:* since bodies are rich material, "use people as
source" is the first idea anyone has. It is probably right, but the lazy version is
worthless. What makes it work is a specific, limited, defensible-sounding form of it that a
decent person could sign off on — and the fact that it does not read as murder to the
people doing it.

**2. The Antagonist. Give me three candidate versions.**
He achieved his current state by cutting something out of himself. Tell me what he removed,
what he became, and what he still wants that proves the removal failed. His goal must be
sympathetic if you squint — many readers should privately agree with him for a chapter.
What he removed must be a real, operable component the system recognizes, and the procedure
must be rule-legal: anyone with his knowledge and nerve could do it, and the reason almost
nobody does is the interesting part. He is not sealed, not prophesied, and gets no stronger
across the story — if anything he is slowly becoming less able.

**3. His lieutenants — 7 of them**, each an externalized piece of him. Not a sin-list unless
sins are thematically load-bearing. Use whatever taxonomy the world already implies —
relations, seasons, castes, tenses, trades, stages of a craft. Give each one a death that
argues against the thing it embodies: the manner of dying must refute its own premise.
Nobody dies to a bigger spell. Several should die without the protagonists killing them; at
least one dies by being given exactly what it wanted; at least one dies because a piece
cannot do a thing only a whole person can do. Order the deaths so the sequence is a rising
argument, not a rising power curve.

**4. The Apparatus.** How the nation itself is the machine — geography, infrastructure or
institution as ritual hardware, running for generations in plain sight and doing genuinely
useful ordinary work. The people maintaining it are mostly not conspirators; the horror is
competence, not conspiracy. Include two historical events the reader will initially accept
as ordinary history and later recognize as construction steps. For each: the schoolbook
version, the detail that never fit, what it actually accomplished, and the moment it flips.

**5. The Righteous Enemy.** A survivor of one of those events who hunts the protagonists
using the same forbidden art, with a grievance the story never fully refutes. Not the
antagonist's servant, not secretly good, not converted. They use the loophole knowingly and
are honest about who pays, by a rule they follow rather than an appetite. At the end the
reader should still think they were substantially right and be unable to say cleanly what
they should have done instead.

**6. The Small Atrocity.** One intimate, non-spectacular horror early on, involving someone
with no strategic importance — not a mentor, not a relative, not a plot key, never avenged.
It must be a correct application of the system, not a villain's cruelty. Nobody in the scene
is sadistic; the person doing it may be decent and apologetic and following a defensible
procedure. The reader must follow the reasoning and find no error in it. Quiet and
procedural — no gore, no torture, no child harmed for shock.

**7. The Refused Shortcut.** The endgame choice where the protagonist wins by surrendering
the power. The shortcut must be real and genuinely work — not a temptation that curdles on
contact. The refusal must defeat the antagonist thematically: the argument he built his life
on is demonstrated false in front of him by an action, not a speech, and there must be a
mechanism by which a demonstrated argument actually costs him. Show what the surrender costs
(at least four things, permanent, including one the reader was rooting for and one that
hurts someone who did not get a vote) and what it does not fix (at least five, including the
dead and the political reality). Some things get worse.

## HARD CONSTRAINTS

- No chosen-one prophecy. No sealed dark lord. No power-scaling escalation — the endgame is
  not a bigger fight.
- The antagonist is defeated by a thematic argument the story has been building since page
  one.
- **No Fullmetal Alchemist iconography.** Specifically banned: the word *alchemy*;
  transmutation circles, arrays, or any drawn figure as the casting method; a philosopher's
  stone or any condensed red soul-object; homunculi; seven-deadly-sins naming; a
  Truth/Gate/portal; a toll paid in limbs; prosthetic-limb culture; state-certified
  alchemist ranks; a genocide-fueled battery; a being from beneath the world who taught
  humanity the art as a long con.
- **Conservation IS the law and I want it** — but the phrase "equivalent exchange" is
  banned, and so is stating conservation as a bare slogan about gain and price. The
  originality is carried by the two-axis system: material and energy are separate
  constraints with separate failure modes, and the three failure modes above are yours to
  make specific. Nothing else in the world may descend from FMA.
- Avoid reskinning FFVII's Lifestream, Mistborn, Avatar's bending, Nasuverse magecraft, or
  Warhammer's Warp. Do not name the energy mana, aether, chi, essence, or mako.

## REGISTER — READ THIS TWICE

**Banned entirely: the language of accounting, law and bureaucracy.** No ledgers, debts,
arrears, billing, charges, credit, collateral, tenure, probate, filings, clerks, courts,
audits, seals, dockets, statutes, shares, quotas, or a magic system that reads as
paperwork. Nobody's magic is tracked in a book. Nobody is a party to anything. If a
sentence would fit in a solicitor's letter, cut it.

**Use instead the language of MAKING THINGS.** Cooking, brewing, baking, smithing, dyeing,
tanning, glassmaking, medicine, masonry, farming — trades where you combine real materials
and know from the feel of them whether you have enough. That vocabulary fits
transformation-from-material natively, and it is how people who do this for a living would
actually talk about it.

**Keep it simple.** One central metaphor, held consistently. At most ten invented terms in
the entire world, each one earning its place — I should be able to hold the whole vocabulary
in my head after one read. No interlocking systems of custom that need a diagram. If
explaining the loophole takes more than five sentences, it is too complicated. Concrete
nouns, plain prose, no preciousness. Every faction-facing name must be sayable out loud in
two seconds.

## CRAFT

Professional story-bible prose for adults. Concrete and unsentimental. Every rule must have
teeth — it must be able to hurt a sympathetic person, and it should generate grief on its
own without anyone choosing to be cruel. Names must sound like one coherent culture.
