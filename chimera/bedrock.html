Project Chimera · Gate 1 · the whole bedrock in five minutes

The Bedrock

Matter is grain — fine particles in families that never become one another. Every working is two motions: SOLVE parts the hold that keeps an arrangement together, SET re-ties it into a new one. The charge you spend is the hold itself, and it is called sap.

01 · The whole system in one paragraph

A working is an inventory, and it does not stop to ask

You name a product. The product has a composition. You lay a boundary, and inside that boundary is what the working will take. If what's inside matches what the product needs, you get the product. If it doesn't, the working doesn't abandon the job — it finishes out of whatever is nearest that will serve. There is always something nearest.

Brass is copper and zinc, seven to three. So: wire, zinc powder, any shape of brass I like. Not steel — there is no iron in brass. Ask anyway and the working goes and finds iron: the nails in that door, the buckle on my belt. Ask short of lime or water, and the nearest source is the man standing behind me.

Three facts and no others: families never convert · proportion is arithmetic · nothing is discarded and nothing is invented.

02 · The rule every fight inherits

Two clocks, running opposite ways

Once you solve, two timers start. The window is how long the matter will still take a shape you choose. The re-hold is how long it stays dangerous. As the volume grows, the window shrinks and the re-hold grows — so big workings lose steerability fastest and stay open longest. That's precisely what makes them the worst thing to abandon.

04 · Who can do it

Everyone has some

You read composition by touch. It's a real perceptual capacity, it is not hereditary, and nobody is tested and shut out at twelve. The extremes are far apart, but the low end is a slope, not a wall. It splits into two axes that never collapse into one number.

05 · Where the power comes from

The ground is a refinery, not a reservoir

A living body is matter that never settles. At death that unrest leaves into whatever the body is touching — and it is not sap when it arrives. The strata refine it over about nine days. A caster standing over a corpse has nothing.

06 · What a caster actually throws

Nothing. She rearranges the room

There is no fire family and no lightning family. A working can only produce what the room is already made of — so the spectacle is material. A railing becoming a spike in one motion. A flagstone dropping a hand's depth under someone's foot. Plaster coming off the wall edge-on into a face. Hinges taken out of a door from across the room.

It should look less like spellcasting and more like the room turning on you at speed — and getting visibly used up as the fight runs on. By the end of a long fight the space is stripped: no fittings, no rail, a floor a hand lower than it was.

Open question — proposal, not canon

Fire is reachable, but as a technique, not a verb. Set a volume of air rich and give it a spark. It follows cleanly from the rules — but air is the thinnest, hardest thing in the world to read, and you are standing inside the boundary when it goes. That would make it an expert move that mostly kills the people who try it, and the handful who can do it safely are famous for it. Say the word and it's in, or say no and the system stays purely material.

07 · The arithmetic a player will check

What a body actually offers

A reach into a person takes water and lime, because those are the only two things a body holds in bulk. Everything else is a rounding error — which is exactly what constrains the horror to one specific shape.

08 · What you see, in order

One working, start to finish — and the three places it can go wrong

This is a sequence, not a scale. A stroke runs cold while the hold is coming out, and delivers heat when it snaps shut. Every failure is that sequence stopping somewhere, and each one stops somewhere different, which is why an observer with no tooltip can tell them apart.

09 · The Law

Name your source, or it'll name one for you.

Not an incantation — nothing listens. It's a workface line, what a foreman says at the top of a shift in the same breath as check your footing. And the crack is that following it is the atrocity. The Law commands you to name your source. It does not say what a source may be. A caster who names a person and lays the boundary on them has followed it exactly, and the working proceeds correctly. Correct procedure and atrocity are the same act, performed with the same hands, in the same order.

Is this the right path?

Seven load-bearing choices. Each one is expensive to reverse later and cheap to reverse now. The middle column is what I didn't pick, so you can see what you'd be trading for.
