Three ways to cast

Every option below inherits the same grounding and the same two strokes. They differ on one question, and choosing one forecloses the others: is casting a caste, a profession, or a gamble you survive?

Shared — already decided

Matter is grain: fine particles in a limited number of families that never become one another. A working is unbinding and rebinding — SOLVE parts the hold, SET re-ties it. The charge is the hold. It sits in every particle because everything is held; almost none is spendable because nearly all of it is doing structural work. Only matter forced out of rest — faulted, shocked, quench-cooled, crushed or previously worked — carries surplus above what its arrangement needs.

Brass is copper and zinc, seven to three.
So: wire, zinc powder, any shape of brass I like.
Not steel — there is no iron in brass.
Ask anyway and the working goes and finds iron: the nails in that door, the buckle on my belt.
Ask short of lime or water, and the nearest source is the man standing behind me.

Option A

The Read

Access is a sense organ. One in a few thousand perceives composition directly — and the gate shuts at twelve.

The tell on the street
Hands on everything — palm to walls, knuckle to rails — and a flat, still, butcher's look.
Half-set matter
Loosened and abandoned: cold, slumping, taking whatever touches it that it is made of.
An aimed reach
He doesn't attack you. He draws the line so that it includes you.
The dead
A likeness — right face, wrong hands, one blended substance all the way down, and it has never moved.

Where the constraint sits

The charge and the strokes are fine. What is scarce is knowing what is there.

What it costs
The ensemble. The gate is genetic and shuts at twelve, nobody ever becomes a caster, and no ally earns a place at the table by working for it.

Option B

The Passage

Anyone can. Almost nobody survives it. One in a hundred walks away clean, and the rest buy their way in.

The tell on the street
Bared forearms: skin grained like unglazed pottery, and how much of the arm is metal.
Half-set matter
Slack — cold grey matter still letting go, running downhill into cellars and wells.
An aimed reach
He scores the ring heavy on three sides and thin toward the conscripts.
The dead
A figure — a dry mineral casting, a third of their weight, standing in a spreading pool of slack.

Where the constraint sits

Nothing is scarce but survivable throughput — and throughput is purchasable.

What it costs
Direction. Every meter runs one way: the profession is staffed by its own casualties, nothing is ever restored, and every arc bends downhill toward less.

Option D

The Specification

No gift and no organ. The gate is holding a fully specified thing fully specified while someone is trying to kill you.

The tell on the street
They name things aloud under their breath all day, and are grey to the knuckles.
Half-set matter
Parted matter that eats only what it matches: half-set iron takes your buckles, not your hand.
An aimed reach
He runs the chalk past his work and under your feet, then asks for more than he brought.
The dead
A likeness — averaged inside, worst at the hands and face, broken up with hammers by the hazard crew.

Where the constraint sits

Blur is not weakness. It is width — and width is what takes in the person standing behind you.

What it costs
Spectacle. Mastery buys speed and coverage, never size, so hour forty looks like hour two in a more interesting room — and at the top everyone knows everything, so elite characters homogenise.

The fork

A is the only option that honours gifted literally — you said “skilled / trained / gifted,” and under A the gate is genetic. D says anyone with a decade and a strong nerve, which makes casting a profession rather than a caste. B splits the difference and charges the body for it.

My recommendation stands at D, with the read grafted on as a trained skill rather than a birth gift — the confidently wrong is exactly the engine Gate 3 asks for, a sincere and correct application of the system that a decent person signs off on with no error in the reasoning to find. And D's stated cost is not a cost: “mastery buys speed and coverage, never size” is a direct satisfaction of your ban on power-scaling escalation.

Still open at this gate — the charge's name (sap / temper / bite) · whether ordinary ground is consumable · what a reach actually takes out of a body (water and lime survives the arithmetic; blood-iron does not) · the astral layer staying out · a families table that is published, disputed and incomplete.
