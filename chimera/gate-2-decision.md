# GATE 2 — THE DECISION

**1. WHICH ARE PLAYABLE — four.**

**Pinch hand.** Moment to moment: thumb-counting a physical pocket board, deciding what leaves your body, and the constant tension of *do I pour now or keep my hands free*. Best onboarding in the set — a loadout screen made of nicks, and no permanent loss. Ship her.

**Carrier.** Moment to moment: walk into contact, palm on the thing, and watch a mark move up your own arm at the instant you spend. Every fight is a question about how much of your remaining forearm this is worth. The most distinct feel in the game and the only one whose resource is a map that shrinks.

**Hafter.** Moment to moment: pure tempo — no counting, no lines, thumb walking bores while you press forward. Easiest to make feel good with a controller, and the only school with a real disarm state (wet strap over the seam and you are a civilian). Also the highest risk of being *too* clean; if the seat's fragility doesn't land, he is a fighting game character in a mining setting.

**Bookhand.** Moment to moment: recon as a mechanic — you spend the night before in the salvage yard putting a district into memory, then walk the level at pace touching four things. Playable *only* if levels reward pre-study; if the game is procedural or arrival-driven, she is a designer's promise you cannot keep, and she should drop to enemy-only.

**2. ENEMY-ONLY — the ground-walker.**

Her power is twenty years on forty acres, which a travelling protagonist can never accumulate and a player will never wait for. As an enemy she buys three things nothing else does: a **fortress** — a stayer who cannot be beaten where she lives and is irrelevant a mile away, which turns a boss fight into a decision about whether to go there at all; a **weather event** — the only silhouette that makes the floor itself hostile, so a room becomes the enemy; and the **aimed reach**, which is the setting's worst accident used deliberately, and it should be something done *to* the player before it is ever a tool. Keep hire-walkers as hireable NPCs so players can borrow the fantasy without owning it.

**3. WHICH ONE THE PROTAGONIST STARTS IN.**

**Candidate A — the pinch hand.** Opening hours: pockets, weight, a pre-fight repack, and a world you learn by milling it. Fights are inventory management with fire in them. The arc is *supply*: you get better by knowing more families and carrying less of them, and the game turns on the day the board goes in a river. Low emotional cost, high systems teaching, travels anywhere. It is the safe start and the weaker story.

**Candidate B — the carrier.** Opening hours: you have nothing and you can do it right now. The first ten hours are the most powerful the player will ever feel — catch a blade bare-handed and hand it back hollow — and the mark moves a thumb's width every time, on screen, at the moment you spend. The arc is *the shrinking map*: forearm to forearm to thigh to back, four sites in a lifetime, and the collarbone as a line the player can choose to cross. Power is fun and then it is terrible, in the same limb, on a gauge.

**Recommendation: start in the carry.** It is the only school where the game's own thesis is the health bar, and the pinch hand is the perfect second school — the thing you pick up when you finally understand why nobody should have to be the channel.

**4. DECISIONS NOT ON THAT LIST THAT ARE EXPENSIVE TO REVERSE.**

- **Paper is an index, not a library.** If a later gate lets the record enter library directly, every school's ceiling moves and the bookhand stops being a class.
- **Irreversibility is a promise.** If hardening can ever be healed — a relic, a surgeon, a late-game item — the carry has no arc and the whole overdraw vocabulary goes soft. Decide now, not at Gate 6.
- **Topology is fixed per character, or it isn't.** Whether a player can be seated, buy a flask, or squat on a pool decides if schools are classes or loadouts. Everything above assumes classes.
- **The aimed reach belongs to one school.** It is assigned to the walker. If a second school gets it, the crack in the law is spent before Gate 3 opens.
