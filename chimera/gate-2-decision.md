# GATE 2 — THE DECISION

## 1. Which schools are playable

**Recommend three at start, one unlocked, one enemy-only.**

**Take-off hands — playable.** Moment to moment: you are moving, upright, talking, spending a flask you can count, taking buildings apart at the joints. The fun is homework that pays out instantly — learn to read a hinge and a strange room is already library. The risk you feel constantly is that you cannot check anything you believe.

**Quicks — playable.** Moment to moment: closing distance you are not supposed to have, banking a lump of half-set with your off hand on a ninety-second clock while your working hand is committed, and a jar-lid flicked with a thumb. The best off-hand resource loop in the set.

**Matchers — playable.** Moment to moment: two hands doing different jobs, thumb reading nicks blind, arithmetic said out loud, and a real loadout decision before every fight in the ready row. This is the inventory school and it is the one that rewards hoarding.

**Rods — playable, but bought, not chosen.** Moment to moment: tap, give ground, read, and win the twentieth second. Playing a rod means paying a seating fee and accepting a permanent hole in the body — that is a progression event, not a character-creation checkbox. Make it a mid-game doctrine the player elects into, with a one-in-six failure that is real.

**Surveyors — enemy-only.** Moment to moment it is three days of preparation and then a fight you have already won, which is thrilling to lose to and dull to play. Off prepared ground she is the weakest thing in the game, so a playable surveyor forces every mission into the same shape.

## 2. Enemy-only, and what it buys

**Surveyors** give us the fight that is a building: the player walks into a house that has been drawn on and has to find the lines, break one, and watch the room turn. It teaches the counter-survey — *the best thing to find in a house is somebody else's chalk* — which is the most teachable clever move in the setting. It also gives us the only pool antagonists, which means fights with a fixed centre, which means objectives rather than health bars.

If we want a second enemy-only school for budget reasons, it is **rods**, and we lose less than it looks: their silhouette reads instantly from outside and their counterplay (hit the arm, wet the strap) is the most satisfying thing in the roster to be taught by getting it done to you first.

## 3. Where the protagonist starts

**Candidate A — take-off hand.** Opening hours are a town, a chalk stub, and learning what a date stone means. Power arrives as knowledge and is immediately enormous: you walk into a room you have never entered and it is already yours. The arc is authorship. She asserts things about the world and the world pays for her being right; then the record is older than the people in the room, and the drag on the floor points at her, and there is no version where she says it was not her.

**Candidate B — quick.** Opening hours are hands on people: a break set in nine-tenths of a second, a man who gets up. Power arrives as care, and it is the most valuable thing anyone on a field can do. The arc is complicity. She learns that a reach is aimable, that nobody can tell an aimed one from a dumb one afterward, and that the workface line commands her to name a source and does not say what a source may be.

**Recommendation: start as a take-off hand.** Hour one teaches the access gate itself — recognition is earned by looking at a hinge, not granted by a stat — and the turn is delivered by the world rather than by the player's own choice, which lets her be capable and complicit before she has agreed to anything. Bring the quick in as the second doctrine at mid-game, when the player has something to lose by learning what she learns.

## 4. Decisions not on that list that are expensive to reverse

**State comes free with recognition.** This gate rules that on a material already in library, state resolves at recognition speed at any acuity. Reverse it and both state-led schools become strong-acuity-only, the disreputable school becomes the most natally gated one in the game, and *acuity is not a power stat* stops being true.

**An instrument buys depth and nothing else.** If a rod's instrument ever buys speed or state as well, acuity becomes purchasable outright and the rod trade's whole class politics — money and nerve standing in for birth — collapses into a shop.

**Nobody owns substitution as a school; the surveyors own it as a practice.** If a later gate spins the substitution fixer out into his own faction, the surveyors lose both their out-edges, the graph goes back to being a ladder with survey at the bottom, and we will have to rebuild the counters from scratch.

**A wall's worth of half-set stands for a day and a half.** The surveyor's battery, her trap, and her silhouette all rest on that single canon number. Change the mass-to-time curve and the school has no object.

**Bare skin is the access gate.** The moment anything exists that reads through a glove, four of five schools lose their cold-and-wet weakness at once.
