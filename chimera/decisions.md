## GATE 1 — in progress

### Decided

**Which stroke is the hard one — SOLVE.**
Loosening is the rare precision act; setting is the follow-through most trained people manage.
Casters read as surgeons. The strokes are not symmetrical, and SET gets its own distinct failure
character: sets do not fail so much as go wrong.
*(Raised off-list under §0's diagnostic rule — it changes how magic feels in the hands more than
the Law's wording does.)*

**Reaching is aimable.**
A caster can deliberately lay a bad boundary so the shortfall is taken from a source of their
choosing. Reaching is therefore the antagonist's working *method*, not only an accident — while
still being able to happen by accident to people who did not intend it.
*(Also raised off-list: cheap to decide now, a bolted-on rule later.)*

**The coinage ruling.**
An "invented term" is a coinage a reader cannot parse from English. Repurposed plain English and
real trade words — slag, grade, rim, seam, dose, residue — are free and are preferred. Coined
faction names count against the cap. Injury stages and units named in plain English are free.
Ten coinages maximum for the entire world; at most three spent at Gate 1.
*(Human delegated this one. Reasoning: §5 caps *invented* terms, and §6 explicitly asks for
repurposed trade vocabulary, so taxing plain English would fight the register the prompt wants.)*

**The technical grounding — particulate.**
Matter is made of something fine and divisible; a trained person unbinds it and rebinds it.
Deconstruct and reconstruct. The player must be able to reason from the grounding the way an
audience reasons from alchemy in *Fullmetal Alchemist* — "this is X, X contains Y, therefore I can
make Z and cannot make W." The grounding is the source of the system's credibility, not a flavour
applied to a generic resource bar.
*Human's words: "alchemy made sense for FMA. those don't make sense. thinking Quantum or like the
superhero girl from Invincible that uses her power over atoms to make things, something similar to
that for deconstruct/reconstruct, quantumly."*

**The shape of the charge.**
- Baked into the universe, present in every particle of matter. It is everywhere.
- Pulled out of the Earth using specialised extractors — industrial equipment, a real trade.
- Old-world objects and relics carry it in abundance. There was a prior age; its leavings are the
  richest sources known and nobody can make more of them.
- Storable in augments — worn, carried or implanted, up to and including replaced limbs.
  Augmentation is a respectable branch of the trade.
- Access is rare. Most people are ordinary and have no powers at all.
- Wisdom and understanding are the progression axis. Comprehension is the power curve, not raw
  output.

### Open

- **Why access is rare.** The perceptual gift, the survivable passage, the controlled instrument, or
  the hard-won understanding. Round 3 is building all four.
- **The name of the charge.** Two rounds have died here. It must sound like what a working
  professional calls the stuff in their hands.
- **The exact wording of the Law**, and the loophole that follows from that exact wording.
- **How deep the technical literacy goes** — does this culture have an atomic theory and talk like
  chemists, or work empirically by grade, purity and family the way real 18th-century chemistry did?
- **The astral stance** — in, and how far, or out.

### The reconciliation any answer must survive

If the energy is in every particle of everything, it cannot also be scarce — yet map control must be
power, rich zones must exist, supply lines must matter, and relics must be worth killing for. The
working answer is bound versus free: it is in everything but held tight, and the whole difficulty is
getting it out. That has to be specific and mechanical — what makes it loosely held here and locked
there, what an extractor physically does, and why old-world relics give it up so readily.
