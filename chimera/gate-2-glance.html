The Five Schools

A school is not a spell list and not a faction. It is the answer to one question: what do you cast with. Everyone obeys the same two strokes — but the substance a working eats has to come from somewhere, and there are only so many places to get it: what you brought, what you’re holding, what you are, what you remember, or what’s under your feet.

The five

How a person reads — cutting a chip, sounding through a bar, walking ground the night before — is now personal style inside a school, never the thing that defines it. Two casters of the same school can work completely differently and both be orthodox.

Pinch hand

Casts withStock she milled and packed herself

She never asks a wall what it is, because a wall can lie and her own sieve cannot. Everything she does subtracts — and when the board runs out she works the room at a novice’s speed with no fast tier at all.

Why you would pick itBring everything. Never be wrong.

What kills itCannot pour with her hands full. The pour is the tempo, one per exchange, and she is defenceless through it.

Hafter

Casts withThe instrument, and the arm that is one

The man who touches nothing. He works at the far end of a rod, with a boundary scored permanently into it, and no count and no line — for as long as the seat holds.

Why you would pick itDuellist tempo. Nobody counting swallows.

What kills itHe works what he never touches, so he is state-blind — and half-set eats instruments, then travels up the shaft into the port at his wrist.

Carrier

Casts withHerself. The boundary is cut into her skin

She never draws a line because she is one. Nothing in her hands, nobody with her, works the instant she can touch the thing — and the sap crosses her flesh to get there.

Why you would pick itDo it now, with nothing.

What kills itThe cost never resets. Every other caster’s bill clears and hers does not.

Bookhand

Casts withThe made world, known by class and vintage

Walks through a building at an ordinary pace touching nothing, talking to herself in proportions. She knew what was in the wall before she arrived.

Why you would pick itKnow the building before you get there.

What kills itNothing natural, worked or weathered. And she cannot check — doubt costs her half a minute and returns families.

Ground-walker

Casts withThe ground, and pools nobody surveyed

Works from underneath, on ground she has covered on her own knees until it is in library, drawing off pools she found herself — no gate, no basin, no permission.

Why you would pick itOwn the floor everyone is standing on.

What kills itShe can make no thing whatsoever. No brass, no wire, no patched armour — she takes things apart and builds nothing.

The carrier’s two clocks

The one you asked for, and it came back better than specified. Her cost runs on two separate gauges, and knowing which is which is the whole of the school’s arithmetic.

Who beats whom

Twelve edges over five schools — which makes it a complete graph: every pair fights, nobody is unbeaten, nobody is a designated victim. Two pairs run both ways and flip on terrain: pinch↔carrier turns on who prepared the room, pinch↔walker on whose ground it is.

A few edges worth knowing, because they are the ones that teach the system: the walker beats the pinch hand because she aims the reach, and the most out-of-rest matter in the room is the powder on the pinch hand’s chest. The carrier beats the hafter because steel is deep in her library and she closes the ring on the rod itself. The bookhand beats the walker without ever approaching — she has the district’s gate-work in the record and shuts a gate two streets away.

Your call

Decision 1

Which are playable

Recommended — four playable

Pinch hand, carrier, hafter, bookhand. Each has a genuinely different hand: thumb-counting a pocket board · walking into contact · pure tempo with no count · recon as a mechanic.

Caveat on the bookhand

She is playable only if levels reward pre-study. If the game is procedural or arrival-driven, she is a promise you cannot keep and should drop to enemy-only.

Decision 2

Ground-walker enemy-only?

Recommended — enemy-only

Her power is twenty years on forty acres, which a travelling protagonist can never accumulate. As an enemy she buys a fortress (unbeatable where she lives, irrelevant a mile away — so the boss fight becomes a decision about whether to go there), a weather event (the only silhouette that makes the floor itself hostile), and the aimed reach — which should be done to the player long before it is ever a tool.

Keep hire-walkers hireable

The trade’s other half goes ground to ground for money. Hire them as NPCs and players borrow the fantasy without owning it.

Decision 3

Where the protagonist starts

Recommended — the carrier

You have nothing and you can do it right now. The first ten hours are the most powerful the player will ever feel — catch a blade bare-handed, hand it back hollow — and the mark moves a thumb’s width every time, on screen, at the moment you spend. The arc is the shrinking map. It is the only school where the game’s own thesis is the health bar.

Alternative — the pinch hand

Pockets, weight, a pre-fight repack, a world you learn by milling it. High systems teaching, low emotional cost, travels anywhere. The safe start and the weaker story — and the perfect second school, the thing you pick up when you finally understand why nobody should have to be the channel.

Decision 4 — expensive to reverse

Four rulings made for you

Irreversibility is a promise

If hardening can ever be healed — a relic, a surgeon, a late-game item — the carrier has no arc and the whole overdraw vocabulary goes soft. Decide now, not at Gate 6.

Paper is an index, not a library

A record tells you which of the things you have already touched this is. If a later gate lets the record enter library directly, the bookhand stops being a class.

A chain contains a solve

There is no cheap fast tier. Every school’s FAST costs a full solve and a quarter, so the fast tier is the expensive tier for everybody, and most work is done on the plain set.

The aimed reach belongs to one school

It is the walker’s. If a second school gets it, the crack in the Law is spent before Gate 3 opens.
