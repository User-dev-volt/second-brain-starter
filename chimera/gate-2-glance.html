The Five Schools

A school is what you cast with. Not a faction, not a culture, not a nation — those are Gate 5. This is the class system: what's in your hands, what it looks like across a room, and what beats you. None of the five is a tier.

The five

Casts with · carried reagents

The bag

Flint, carbon, salts, filings — a grab bag she put together herself and grinds fresh out of whatever she can scavenge. Pouches, a working apron, and a scale: she weighs before she names. On a march she walks where nobody wants to walk near her.

Great atEverything in her pouches is stock she has already read, so it comes up FAST — and she bought the proportion, so she is never short by accident.

What kills itSomebody else's hand in her stores. Run her dry and she's a novice in her own room — she knows her pouches by heart and has never had to learn a wall. And the accelerant on her hip is the nearest source in the room: four metres on a fast stroke, twelve on a heavy one.

Casts with · objects

The instrument

A rod, a gauntlet, armour — and at the far end, metal that replaced a limb. Only ever a thing he has already read through. Gloved hands, and the habit of working at arm's length: the tool touches the material so he doesn't have to.

Great atA line that cannot be scuffed out — scored into the metal, so nobody smudges it from six feet. And he packs the rod in his own time, so he opens a fight already holding the stuff for it.

What kills itA swapped capsule. He sets what he thinks he packed. He can buy a line that never smudges; he can never buy acuity.

Casts with · herself

The body

The line is cut into her own skin, so she is always inside her own boundary — and anything inside the line is a source. Every caster's line shows dust-fine when the stroke fires; hers is the only one you can read with no stroke thrown. Scored, healed, and sitting on her arm.

Great atNothing to drop and nothing to take off her — she walks into a room already armed. What she's armed with is two things: water and lime, and they are always there.

What kills itShe is inside every line she draws, so every working she throws short takes her own water and lime first — cramps, then teeth, then bone, and it never comes back. And a scored line is permanent: a new one crossing an old one breaks the run before it closes, so she can never write on the same skin twice. Her map only shrinks — forearm, other forearm, thigh, back. Nobody lets it past the collarbone.

Casts with · memory

The scholar

What she has already read and retained. Paper is only an index — it tells her which of the things she has touched this is. Empty hands and a sample case, and she has already touched the wall on her way in.

Great atRecognising. Library drops a working from HEAVY to FAST, and nobody's list is longer — so more of any room is FAST for her than for anyone else standing in it.

What kills itSubstitution. Swap the fittings and she works at full speed, confidently, on a material that isn't there. Then she finds out — and now she has to read the thing and disprove herself, which is slower than a novice.

Casts with · instinct

The attuned

Not a trade; a people. She has been reading by touch since she could walk. No pouch, no rod, nothing anyone taught her. Hands on the ground, and she stays down longer than anyone thinks she needs to.

Great atState. A palm tells her whether a thing is at rest, forced out of rest, or already worked. On ground she has knelt on for twenty years she feels the surplus coming up slower than it did — which is how a stolen basin upstream gets noticed a season before anyone's well drops.

What kills itNo library. Recognising is the whole of the FAST tier, and large means chained, and chained means from memory — so everything she takes has to fit inside one working.

Who beats whom

The bag ⇄ the body

The bag can start a working and walk off it, and a floor of half-set matter is the safest room the body will ever stand in — every shortfall she throws feeds on the mass instead of on her. Right up to the moment she has to put a palm down. And the body works short on purpose standing next to the pouch, so the working takes the powder off the nearest hip.

The scholar ⇄ the attuned

A door is only takeable by someone who has already touched what it's made of — and off her own parish the attuned has touched almost nothing, while the scholar's list runs to every fitting in the county. But the attuned picks the fight on her own ground, where twenty years of kneeling is her whole library and the scholar's list is no longer than anyone else's.

The bag → the scholar

The bag has the stock to change a room before anyone fights in it — a thin plate glued over the door fittings, a tenth part of something else mixed through the stores. A long list is worse than no list once it's wrong.

The instrument → the attuned

Everything on him is tinned, painted and greased, so her read stops at the first seam and she has to take a second look. That's two seconds — and two seconds is her standing still in front of a man with a loaded rod.

The instrument → the bag

His supply is already inside the thing in his hand; hers is in pouches on a strap. Cut the strap and she's grinding scavenge while he's still firing what he packed.

The body → the instrument

Everything he casts with can be swapped, fouled or pulled off him. She has nothing on her to take.

Your call

Five open questions. The names, the axis and the four-slot card are settled — these are the ones I'd rather you decided than have me quietly decide by writing the long document a particular way.

The questionWhich are playable, and which are enemy-only?

What I'd doAll five playable. None of them is a tier, and the counter graph works because every school is somebody's answer

What it costsThe attuned is the hardest to build a kit for, because she has no fast tier and no chaining. If she's playable, she needs a reason to be picked

The questionWhere does the protagonist start?

What I'd doThe bag. It's the school that teaches the system — inventory, proportion, a resource that visibly deletes, and a restock loop between fights

What it costsIt's also the school carrying the nearest source in the room on her hip, so the first time the Law bites, it bites her. That may be exactly right, or it may be too early

The questionIs the attuned currently the weakest design?

What I'd doShe takes the two hardest caps in the system — no fast tier, no large working ever — for a sense Gate 1 says is just the top of the ordinary acuity scale. The proposed fix: stop paying her in perception, pay her in the reach. She's the only caster who can work short on purpose and be certain what the working will take; everyone else's reach is a gamble

What it costsThat's a new mechanic, which is why it isn't in the cards above. It needs no bloodline, no second sense and no extra sap — but it is still a Gate 2 addition and it's yours to approve, not mine to slip in

The questionHow much of the attuned belongs to Gate 5?

What I'd doHer mechanics stay here; her people goes to Gate 5. The card says "a people" and then stops

What it costsIt leaves the most interesting thing about her unwritten for now. The alternative — explaining her here — is exactly how attempt three turned into ethnography

The questionDoes the counter graph hold?

What I'd doShip it. It's asymmetric on purpose: two mutual pairs, four pairs with no edge, and the instrument sitting off to one side with two wins and one loss

What it costsPush on the bag against the body if you push anywhere. It's the closest thing to a designed matchup rather than a derived one, and if you don't buy it, the honest fix is to give the body her loss somewhere else rather than pretend she has one
