# GATE 3 — THE LOOPHOLE. Three forms. Decide one.

**Nothing is built until you pick.** Per the prompt: *"Decide with the human: the form of the
loophole before anything is built on it."* The intimate horror is written **after** — it has to be a
correct application of whichever form you choose, or it is just a villain being cruel.

---

## What the brief demands of the answer

- It follows from the Law's exact wording with the inevitability of a real loophole.
- **It is impossible to perform alone.** It structurally requires other people to pay.
- It is **specific, limited, and defensible-sounding** — a decent person can sign off on it, and it
  does not read as murder to the people doing it. "Use people" is the lazy version and is worthless.
- Explaining it takes **five sentences or fewer**.

## The mechanical fact all three are built on

Gate 1 §5: *"the working takes the whole shortfall from one source if that source holds enough, and
moves outward to a second only if the first is exhausted."* So **shortfall is a dial.** A working
takes only what it is short of. Put down a measured amount less material than the job needs, and the
working goes and finds exactly that much and no more. The trade already meters this precisely — it
grades accelerant and doses stock. Being short by a known quantity is a professional skill, not an
accident. That is the crack, and it is why the loophole is inevitable rather than invented.

---

## OPTION A — The rota. *Nobody is harmed.*

**The five sentences.** A working takes only what it is short of, and shortfall is a dial: lay down a
measured amount less than the job needs and the working finds exactly that much, from the nearest
source that holds it. Set the shortfall at a cup of water and a spoon of lime, and the nearest source
can be a person — she holds vastly more than that, replaces the water in a day and the lime over a
few weeks, and walks home the same evening. So the works stands one volunteer at the caster's
shoulder, names her aloud as the source, pays her a half-day, and runs a rota so nobody stands twice
in a shift. It follows the Law to the letter, it is witnessed, it is written in a book, and it is the
only way a person with no gift at all can be worth something to a working. And the same dial that
makes one cup safe is the dial you turn when the job is bigger, the rota is short, and there is a
roof coming down.

**What it makes possible.** An institution, not an incident: standing lists, rates, a register of who
stood and when, works that write it into a contract, and a whole class of people whose contribution
to the trade is their own water. It lands Gate 2's class fact — the sap economy is not a casters'
economy — directly on the body of the person with no gift. Every horror it produces is aggregate: the
man who takes an extra turn for the extra half-day, the week the rota runs short, the register nobody
adds up. It is the version where **the reader follows the reasoning and finds no error in it**,
because there isn't one in any single line — only in the column.

**What it costs.** No single act is an atrocity, which is the design and also the risk: the horror has
to be assembled by the reader out of arithmetic, and if the story doesn't do that work it reads as a
mildly grim blood drive. It also puts a genuinely humane institution at the centre of a dark setting,
which is a bet on restraint.

---

## OPTION B — The declared. *They were going anyway.*

**The five sentences.** A body two days dead is the densest thing in most rooms, and the trade
already works beside its own dead for exactly that reason. A person in their last day is denser
still, and holds water and lime they will not live to use. So the hopeless — the ones the surgeons
have already set aside — are laid inside the boundary, named aloud as the source, and a working that
would otherwise have gone short takes what they had left. It is done with their word where there is a
word to give, the surgeon signs the declaration, and everyone involved will tell you it shortened a
bad death rather than caused one. The exploit is not the caster's; it is the form the surgeon signs,
and the only question that was ever really at issue is who counts as already dying.

**What it makes possible.** The atrocity sits in paperwork rather than in hands, which makes it
prosecutable, deniable, and expandable — the horror is a definition widening one clause at a time.
Gives the field hospital and the front their own economy and hands Gate 5 an apparatus.

**What it costs.** It is the most conventional of the three and the reader sees the slope from the
first page, so the reveal has less to give. It also borrows heavily against Gate 5 — it needs a state,
a hospital corps and a declaring authority that do not exist yet — and it edges toward the villainy
the brief explicitly rules out: *nobody in the scene is sadistic.*

---

## OPTION C — The named ground. *Nobody named a person.*

**The five sentences.** The Law says name your source; it does not say the source must be a thing you
can point at. A caster can lay the boundary on a place — the yard, the wall, the bunkhouse — and name
the place, correctly and out loud, and the working takes the nearest sufficient source inside it.
In a good building that is the lime in the plaster and the water in the cistern, and the building
pays. In a lath-and-board tenement there is nothing in the room that holds enough except the people
in it. Nobody nominated anybody, the naming was accurate, the man who signed the boundary was looking
at a map — and the arithmetic selects the poor, because the arithmetic is about what your walls are
made of.

**What it makes possible.** The cruellest true fact available to this world: the harm is distributed
by building material, which is to say by class, and no one chose it. It preserves the Law perfectly
and it makes the horror structural rather than moral.

**What it costs.** Nobody has agency, so nobody can be indicted, and the brief wants a decent person
who *signs off* — here nobody quite does. It also erodes Gate 1's single most important legibility
rule, *"everyone in the room knows who paid,"* by arranging for there to be no one in the room.

---

## Why these are exclusive

Whichever one the world has normalised makes the other two read as crimes. A world with the rota
prosecutes the declared. A world with the declared has no use for a rota. A world that names ground
never developed the vocabulary of consent at all.

## Recommendation

**A — the rota**, because it is the only one of the three that is structurally impossible alone, that
a decent person signs off on without flinching, and whose horror is arithmetic rather than villainy.

**Open, and yours:** what the practice is called. *Standing* is the plain-English candidate — *"she
stood for the works on Tuesday"* — but Gate 2 already uses *standing* for a caster's reputation, and
that collision is either the best thing in the gate or a confusion. Say which.
