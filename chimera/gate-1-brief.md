# GATE 1 — THE BEDROCK (round 3)

## SHARED — already decided, all options inherit this

Matter is **grain**: fine particles in a limited number of families that never become one another.
A working is unbinding and rebinding — SOLVE parts the hold, SET re-ties it.

**The charge *is* the hold.** That is the reconciliation. It is in every particle because everything
is held; almost none is spendable because nearly all of it is doing structural work. Only matter
forced out of rest — faulted, shocked, quench-cooled, crushed or previously worked — carries surplus
above what its arrangement needs. An extractor is a mill that fatigues rock until its holds part
faster than they re-form, and catches the release before it re-holds. Relics were set by better
hands: surplus behind a small lock, which is why a touch bleeds them and why nobody living can make more.

**The worked line, reproducible by a player unaided:**

> *Brass is copper and zinc, seven to three. So: wire, zinc powder, any shape of brass I like. Not
> steel — there is no iron in brass. Ask anyway and the working goes and finds iron: the nails in
> that door, the buckle on my belt. Ask short of lime or water, and the nearest source is the man
> standing behind me.*

## THE OPTIONS — choose one; each forecloses the rest

The question they answer: **is casting a caste, a profession, or a gamble you survive?**

**A — THE READ. Access is a sense organ.** One in a few thousand perceives composition directly,
family by family, down to proportion. Acuity is born, naming is taught, the trade is the marriage of
the two. It makes *deception* the primary counter to a caster — facing, loading, dazzle-plates, all
owned by ordinary soldiers — and makes speed a property of the read, so casters walk the ground
beforehand and touch everything. **It costs** the ensemble: the gate is genetic and shuts at twelve,
nobody ever *becomes* a caster, and no ally earns a place at the table by working for it.

**B — THE PASSAGE. Anyone can; almost nobody survives it.** The charge runs from the ground
*through the practitioner*, and living tissue is the loosest thing it can settle into. One in a
hundred walks away clean. Augments are conduits — already-spent matter with nothing left to drop —
so a replaced arm genuinely outperforms a real one, and augmentation is equipment rather than
sacrifice. **It costs** direction: every meter runs one way, the profession is staffed by its own
casualties, and every arc bends downhill toward less.

**D — THE SPECIFICATION. You can only unbind what you can name exactly.** No gift and no organ. The
gate is holding a fully specified thing fully specified while someone is trying to kill you. Blur
does not weaken a working; it *widens* it. This produces the trade's signature monster — the
confidently wrong, smooth and fast and sincere, whose every working lands like a shell. **It costs**
spectacle: mastery buys speed and coverage, never size, so a fight at hour forty looks like one at
hour two in a more interesting room; and at the top everyone knows everything, so elite characters
homogenise.

## THE NAME OF THE CHARGE

**Quick** is out — it collides with *fast* in a three-speed system, and quicklime is a reagent here.
**Flux** and **charge** read as placeholders. Three survive:

- **SAP** — you *tap* it out of things; sappers undermine walls for a living; "the ground's sapped out" explains itself to anyone. Two trades in one syllable, works as noun, verb and adjective, and drawing sap is literally what an extractor does.
- **TEMPER** — smiths *draw the temper* out of steel by heating it and letting it settle, which is exactly the extractor's operation. "Tempered ground" reads instantly. Costs one permanent joke.
- **BITE** — how hard matter grips itself: the mechanism as the name. A file has bite, acid has bite. Costs a live verb collision.

## RECOMMENDATION

**Take D, and graft the read onto it as a trained skill rather than a birth gift** — because the
confidently wrong is the exact engine Gate 3 asks for (a sincere, correct application of the system
that a decent person signs off on, with no error in the reasoning to find), and because D's stated
cost — mastery buys speed and coverage, never size — is not a cost at all but a direct satisfaction
of the ban on power-scaling escalation.

I differ from my own analysts here; they recommended A. The tension you should weigh: you said
"skilled / trained / **gifted**," and A is the only option that honours *gifted* literally. D says
anyone with a decade and a strong nerve. That is the fork — caste or profession — and it is yours.

## DECISIONS THIS GATE NEEDS

1. **The access gate.** Wrong here and the schools, the counterplay and the antagonists all rebuild from nothing.
2. **The wording of the Law.** Muttered a thousand times, and its loophole is the villain's licence. A Law with no crack in it turns aimed reaching into a crime instead of a method.
3. **The name of the charge.** Every sentence in the world contains it.
4. **How deep the technical literacy goes.** All four candidates propose empirical, not atomic: families, grades and provenance, no atoms. The live question is whether a table of the families is published. With none, players cannot reason and we are back at round two; with a complete one, progression becomes memorisation and the comprehension axis collapses. I recommend one that is published, disputed and incomplete.
5. **The astral stance.** All four say out. Ratify it — if a far side exists it silently becomes the answer to every hard question, and the grounding stops carrying weight.
6. **Off-list, expensive: is ordinary ground consumable?** If a caster can stand in a field and draw indefinitely, extractors, supply lines and map control are decoration.
7. **Off-list, expensive: what does a reach take out of a person?** A body holds about one nail's worth of iron, so blood-iron reaching fails arithmetic the first time a player checks. If it takes **water and lime** it survives scrutiny — and it is far worse, because that is the skeleton.

## GRAFTS AVAILABLE

- **From the passage:** the body as a readable record — grain up one cheek means one unrouted passage, done badly, usually to save somebody; and the field hospital that rings its boundary open toward the amputation bin and has stopped asking the wagon drivers where the surplus comes from.
- **From the stamp (dropped as an option):** licensed sinks and the stamped-out man — thousands who know exactly what they would do with a full flask and have thought about it in bed for six years.
- **From the specification:** the **spotter**, who cannot cast but calls materials aloud; kill him and the caster drops a speed tier.
- **From the specification:** relics as a literature we can no longer read — every one burned to win a corridor fight is a page gone.
